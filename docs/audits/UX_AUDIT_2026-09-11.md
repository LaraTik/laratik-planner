# LaraTik Planner — Full User-Experience Audit

**Audit date:** 2026-09-11  
**Audited baseline revision:** `c90ad0db757bc3a35f4e771c6ef70a50d982cdbc`  
**Latest remediation revision:** `d2997b46e71823d84965d22f88ba6ac0670a1670`
**Working-tree status:** dirty; pre-existing storage/media changes were present and preserved  
**Audit type:** repository, browser-evidence, visual-contract, accessibility, and heuristic UX review  
**Primary question:** can users understand what the product is showing, what they should do next, why work is blocked, and how to recover?

## Executive verdict

**Verdict: READY FOR CONTINUED DEVELOPMENT WITH UX DEBT — NOT READY FOR A “NO ISSUES” UX SIGN-OFF.**

LaraTik Planner has a strong product and engineering foundation. The core domain model, role boundaries, workflow state machine, bilingual catalog structure, responsive shell, storage isolation, and automated quality harness are mature enough to support continued development.

The experience is generally understandable for experienced agency operators, but it still asks new or occasional users to interpret too much operational language and too many workflow signals. The highest-risk area is the planning-to-publishing journey: users can usually find the right information, but they do not always get one unmistakable next action, a clear explanation of ownership, or a direct route from a blocker to its fix.

The product should not be described as “no issues” until:

- stage-aware next actions are consistently more prominent than future blockers;
- creative brief and publishing surfaces clearly separate required work from optional detail;
- every publishing blocker links to the exact place where it can be resolved;
- manual accessibility, bilingual editorial, UAT, visual, and performance reviews are complete; and
- all evidence is reconciled at one exact clean release revision.

## Findings by priority

### P0 — No confirmed UX P0 found

No reproducible current UX finding was identified that demonstrates data loss, authorization leakage, or an unusable critical journey. This is not a substitute for production UAT.

### P1 — Resolve before independent UX verification

#### UX-01 — Planning detail still exposes too much decision surface at once

**Affected users:** planners, designers, reviewers, publishers, especially new or infrequent users.  
**Evidence:** `docs/ux-redesign-2026-09-08/planning-ux-audit.md`, `docs/production-readiness/PLANNING_UX_AUDIT_2026-09-09.md`, `PlanningDetailShell`, and the workflow-rail implementation.  
**Impact:** the user sees the complete lifecycle but must decide which tab, rail, utility, or action matters now. This increases hesitation and makes the product feel like an operations console instead of a guided workflow.

**Recommendation:** keep `Overview`, `Creative brief`, `Audience copy`, `Assets`, and `Publish` as the primary path. Keep `Preview`, `Activity`, and Discussion secondary. Make current stage, next action, responsible role, and transition outcome the first information users scan.

**Acceptance criteria:** one primary action is visually dominant for each role/state; utilities do not compete with the production path; legacy hashes remain functional; the hierarchy works at 375px, 768px, 1024px, and desktop widths.

#### UX-02 — Readiness information must remain stage-aware

**Affected users:** planners and content owners working on drafts or review-stage items.  
**Evidence:** prior F-01 walkthrough finding in `docs/ux-redesign-2026-09-08/planning-ux-audit.md`; current readiness and next-action implementation in `docs/production-readiness/PLANNING_UX_AUDIT_2026-09-09.md`.  
**Impact:** a draft can feel broken when it shows publishing requirements that cannot yet be completed. Users may fix the wrong thing or assume they own a later-stage dependency.

**Recommendation:** distinguish `Current work`, `Waiting on`, and `Later readiness`. Count only actionable blockers for the current stage in the primary summary. Keep future requirements behind a non-blocking disclosure.

**Acceptance criteria:** drafts lead with completing the brief and submitting for review; design-stage items lead with delivery/review; publish-stage items lead with channel readiness; every waiting state names the responsible role or external dependency.

#### UX-03 — Creative brief fields need clearer required/optional/advanced grouping

**Affected users:** planners, copywriters, designers, and first-time creators.  
**Evidence:** planning audit evidence B1–B4 and the current format-aware editor.  
**Impact:** strategy, creative direction, references, translations, and AI actions appear in one long surface. Empty optional fields can look like incomplete required work.

**Recommendation:** group fields by outcome: `Required to submit`, `Helpful context`, and `Advanced production details`. Show examples and format-specific guidance at the point of use. Keep translations and AI actions progressively disclosed.

**Acceptance criteria:** required fields are identifiable without scanning the full page; optional fields do not appear as errors; content remains format-driven in `formatPayload`; English/Arabic labels, hints, and errors remain in parity.

#### UX-04 — Publishing needs a guided per-channel pre-flight checklist

**Affected users:** publishers, agency managers, and users recovering a failed publication.  
**Evidence:** planning audit evidence E1–E4 and `docs/audits/META_PUBLISHING_READINESS_AUDIT_2026-09-04.md`.  
**Impact:** setup, destination, copy, media, disclosures, approval, and outcome are valid concepts, but their density makes it difficult to know which single fix will unblock a selected channel.

**Recommendation:** present each channel as an ordered checklist: destination, copy/language, media/disclosures, approval, schedule/outcome. Put the fix beside the blocker and preserve server-side readiness validation.

**Acceptance criteria:** every blocker names its resolver; selecting a blocker moves focus to the relevant field or section; failed, skipped, partially published, and retry states explain the next safe action; client-side state cannot bypass server readiness.

#### UX-05 — Shared audience copy versus channel-specific final copy remains easy to misunderstand

**Affected users:** planners, copywriters, publishers, and reviewers.  
**Evidence:** current `messages-panel` copy and mapper contract; planning audit evidence C1–C4 and E4.  
**Impact:** users may edit a channel override expecting to change the shared source, or wonder why published text differs from shared copy.

**Recommendation:** make the relationship persistent: `Shared audience copy` → `Inherited by channel` → `Custom channel override` → `Final version used for publishing`.

**Acceptance criteria:** the source/override relationship is visible in Copy and Publish; overrides are explicit and reversible; translation sidecars remain in `formatPayload.translations[locale]`; mapper, pre-fill, and material-edit behavior remain unchanged.

#### UX-06 — Manual accessibility and real-user UAT remain release blockers

**Affected users:** keyboard-only users, screen-reader users, zoom users, Arabic users, client reviewers, and real-account operators.  
**Evidence:** `docs/production-readiness/ACCESSIBILITY_CHECKLIST.md`, `docs/production-readiness/UAT_RELEASE.md`, and the current `pnpm test:a11y` run.  
**Impact:** axe coverage confirms structural rules but cannot prove that a person can follow the workflow, understand announcements, operate long forms, or recover from real state changes.

**Recommendation:** complete the manual accessibility checklist and separated-account journey. Treat the current WebKit timeout and JSON-manifest race as test-infrastructure follow-up until isolated reruns establish whether they are environmental or application-related.

**Acceptance criteria:** keyboard-only completion works for login, Quick Create, review decision, comment reply, and calendar move; screen-reader names, headings, announcements, dialogs, and focus restoration are signed off; 200% zoom and reduced-motion checks are recorded; all 30 UAT rows have operator/date/environment/result/evidence.

### P2 — Improve before broad adoption

#### UX-07 — Planning filters and operational vocabulary need progressive disclosure

**Evidence:** `src/components/planning/planning-filters.tsx`, `src/app/(app)/app/w/[slug]/planning/filter-describe.ts`, and the planning UX audit evidence for filter density and enum-derived labels.  
**Impact:** expert operators can use the controls, but new users must decode stage, health, risk, density, and ownership terminology before they can narrow work confidently.  
**Recommendation:** keep Search, Status, and Date visible first. Group Stage, Health, Risk, Owner, Channel, Density, and Format under localized `More filters`, with short definitions and a visible active-filter summary.  
**Acceptance criteria:** the default view exposes no more than the primary filters; opening and closing advanced filters preserves URL state; filtered-empty results explain the active criteria and recovery action; all labels and definitions render in English/Arabic without enum-derived English leaks.  
**Priority/dependencies:** P2; depends on the existing planning filter URL contract and catalog key parity.  
**Verification:** unit tests for query-state preservation and active counts; keyboard and RTL checks at 375px and 768px; filtered-empty browser evidence.

#### UX-08 — Empty and waiting states should answer “why, who, what next”

**Evidence:** generic `EmptyState` callers, draft Assets, provider configuration, filtered-empty planning, archived/denied workspace states, and review waiting-state surfaces.  
**Impact:** a user may see that there is no content or that work is waiting without knowing whether the state is expected, who owns the next step, or how to recover.  
**Recommendation:** apply one state contract everywhere: reason, responsible role or dependency, next allowed action, and recovery/help path. Distinguish first-use empty, filtered-empty, waiting, denied, archived, error, and stale-data states.  
**Acceptance criteria:** every audited empty or waiting state names why it exists, who can change it, what action is available now, and what happens next; forbidden states do not expose unsafe actions; stale-data states offer refresh or escalation; copy is localized and direction-safe.  
**Priority/dependencies:** P2; depends on role-aware state data and the shared feedback component contract, but does not require a schema change.  
**Verification:** state-matrix tests for anonymous, client reviewer, workspace manager, agency admin, suspended, and archived contexts; keyboard focus checks; browser evidence for loading, empty, filtered-empty, denied, and stale states.

#### UX-09 — Mobile actions need a final visual pass around fixed navigation and save bars

**Evidence:** `WorkspaceShell`, mobile workflow sheet, sticky Publish/Copy actions, and responsive visual snapshots. The safe-area and action-hierarchy remediation is implemented, but visual sign-off is still open.  
**Impact:** fixed navigation, sheets, and save/publish actions can compete for the same viewport, obscure content, or make focus movement difficult on small screens.  
**Recommendation:** verify safe-area spacing, scroll-to-action behavior, focus visibility, and keyboard interaction where bottom navigation, sticky save bars, sheets, and long forms share the 375px or 768px viewport.  
**Acceptance criteria:** no fixed action obscures editable content or the focused control; the mobile stage selector remains visually separate from operator actions; bottom sheets restore focus and dismiss safely; controls remain usable at 375px, 768px, and 200% zoom.  
**Priority/dependencies:** P2; code remediation is complete in the current branch; depends on the responsive browser matrix and device-safe-area review.  
**Verification:** Playwright screenshots at 375/768px in EN/AR; keyboard-only focus and scroll checks; reduced-motion and 200% zoom review; human visual approval of intentional deltas.

#### UX-10 — Arabic needs editorial review, not only direction switching

**Evidence:** `docs/i18n/CONTRACT.md`, `src/messages/{en,ar}`, catalog parity tests, RTL overflow checks, and the localized content-detail/publishing-label remediation.
**Impact:** structurally correct RTL can still be hard to understand when terminology, sentence length, dates, numbers, errors, or recovery wording are unnatural or visually ambiguous.  
**Recommendation:** conduct native Arabic editorial review across the full planning-to-publishing journey, including long strings, Western digits, dates, percentages, handles, URLs, and error/recovery wording.  
**Acceptance criteria:** every user-facing string in the audited journeys has an approved Arabic translation; terminology follows the glossary; mixed-direction values remain isolated; dates, times, numbers, and percentages use Western digits and the workspace timezone; no clipping or directional ambiguity remains at required widths.  
**Priority/dependencies:** P2; depends on native editorial ownership and the bilingual browser matrix, not on an API or schema change.  
**Verification:** catalog parity gate; native Arabic sign-off; RTL screenshots and keyboard traversal at 375/768/1024/1280/1440+; axe and overflow checks.

#### UX-11 — Performance evidence is insufficient for a “good UX” claim

**Evidence:** existing public local performance baseline and the absence of authenticated, throttled, representative-volume evidence in the production-readiness records.  
**Impact:** a fast empty local workspace does not demonstrate that planners can open, edit, filter, review, or publish efficiently with realistic data and network conditions.  
**Recommendation:** add throttled LCP/FCP/CLS/INP measurements for public and authenticated routes, representative-volume planning/detail interactions, and bundle/image/font checks.  
**Acceptance criteria:** agreed performance budgets are recorded for sign-in, planning, content detail, and publish; measurements include cold/warm loads and realistic data volume; regressions identify route, asset, query, or network cause; results are tied to an exact clean commit and environment.  
**Priority/dependencies:** P2; depends on a stable test dataset, authenticated test accounts, and an agreed measurement environment.  
**Verification:** repeatable Lighthouse/Web Vitals or equivalent runs under documented throttling; interaction traces for search, filter, tab switch, save, and publish readiness; bundle and image/font reports.

#### UX-12 — Visual evidence must be reconciled before snapshot approval

**Evidence:** `docs/visual-parity/CURRENT_SYNC.md`, `docs/production-readiness/SCREEN_PARITY.md`, visual-regression snapshots, and the unfilled human-review log.  
**Impact:** conflicting historical totals make it impossible to tell whether a visual difference is a true regression, an intentional design update, a fixture difference, or stale evidence.  
**Recommendation:** identify the active manifest, run it at one exact clean revision, classify intentional deltas, and record approved deviations with commit SHA.  
**Acceptance criteria:** one manifest and route inventory is declared authoritative; every failed or changed snapshot is classified; intentional changes have a linked design/source rationale; the final result records revision, viewport, locale, browser, fixture version, and independent reviewer outcome.  
**Priority/dependencies:** P2; depends on a clean release candidate, current Stitch synchronization, deterministic fixtures, and an independent reviewer.  
**Verification:** full current visual matrix at 375/768/1024/1280/1440+ in EN/AR across supported browsers; human review of all deltas; no approval based on pass count alone.

## Development roadmap

### Phase 1 — Remove comprehension blockers

1. Finalize stage-aware Overview summaries and future-readiness disclosure.
2. Make the five-step planning detail path the dominant information architecture.
3. Make required/optional/advanced brief grouping explicit.
4. Convert Publish into guided per-channel pre-flight checklists.
5. Persist shared-copy/inherited/override/final-copy explanations.

### Phase 2 — Make every state self-explanatory

1. Standardize empty, waiting, denied, archived, filtered-empty, error, and stale states.
2. Ensure every blocked action names the owner and resolver.
3. Verify mobile sticky actions, sheets, safe areas, and focus restoration.

### Phase 3 — Close trust and evidence gaps

1. Complete manual keyboard, screen-reader, zoom, reduced-motion, and Arabic editorial review.
2. Execute the separated-account 30-step UAT journey.
3. Reconcile and rerun the exact visual matrix.
4. Capture authenticated performance and representative-volume evidence.

## Final acceptance criteria

The UX can be called “good and ready for independent review” when:

- no P0/P1 comprehension, accessibility, privacy, or critical-flow issue remains;
- every main screen has one clear next action or an explicit reason no action is available;
- every blocker explains what is wrong, who owns it, and where to fix it;
- the primary planning-to-publishing journey is understandable without expert interpretation;
- all supported roles see the correct work and no irrelevant internal material;
- English/LTR and Arabic/RTL journeys are editorially reviewed at all required widths;
- manual accessibility, UAT, visual, and performance evidence is complete; and
- evidence is tied to one exact clean commit and independently reviewed.

## Assumptions and limitations

- This audit is based on repository evidence, current source, Stitch references, automated tests, and heuristic human-comprehension review. It is not moderated customer research.
- The current working-tree storage/media changes were not altered. Findings involving those surfaces should be rechecked after commit.
- The current `pnpm test:a11y` run was interrupted after WebKit public-route timeouts; passing checks showed no axe violations, but the complete current matrix remains unproven.
- The current visual run was interrupted after 22 passing cases because the full suite is long-running. Existing current-sync claims and the human visual-review log must be reconciled before release approval.
- Automated passing does not equal independent UX verification.
